Thermal Systems Architecture for LV Cabinet

Hyliion • Generator Platform • Forced Convection • System Curves • CFD • Environmental Filtration

Designed a forced-convection thermal management strategy for a low-voltage (LV) control cabinet on a generator platform. The work focused on keeping cabinet temperatures within industry-standard reliability limits under continuous operation, while respecting tight packaging, filtration-driven pressure drop, and real-world manufacturing constraints.

Problem

  • Thermal risk: without forced convection, internal temperatures can exceed acceptable operating limits for control and power components.
  • Objective: maintain cabinet air temperatures within reliability limits under continuous operation.
  • Environmental constraint: filtered intake required for humid/marine-adjacent operation → higher system pressure drop.
  • Integration reality: airflow must fit within restricted packaging while remaining serviceable and manufacturable.
Reliability Pressure Drop Filtration Packaging DFM

Approach

1) First-principles sizing + converged operating point

Built an iterative sizing workflow to compute the airflow requirement from energy balance, then converged the coupled problem between airflow, pressure drop, fan power, and resulting temperature rise. This avoided “assumption-based” fan sizing and produced a physically consistent operating point.

2) System curve (ΔP vs V̇) using CFD, anchored by measurement

Developed the cabinet system resistance curve to account for internal flow restrictions and filtration losses. Used CFD to capture the geometry-driven losses, and anchored the methodology using instrumentation data from a prior platform configuration to ensure the modeling approach was realistic.

3) Fan selection + cabinet integration

  • Selected the fan at the true operating point by intersecting the system curve with supplier fan performance curves.
  • Checked electrical power availability and thermal impact of fan power as part of the converged solution.
  • Updated cabinet intake/exhaust geometry to improve airflow distribution and integration within packaging limits.
  • Designed with serviceability and manufacturability in mind (access, mounting, filtration replacement).
