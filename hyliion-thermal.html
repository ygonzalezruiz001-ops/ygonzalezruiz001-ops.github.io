Thermal Systems Architecture for LV Cabinet

Hyliion • Generator Platform • Forced Convection • Fan & Filter Selection • Plenum Design • Verification Planning

Developed a forced-convection thermal management architecture for a low-voltage (LV) control cabinet on a generator platform. The work focused on maintaining electronics reliability under worst-case ambient conditions while meeting environmental ingress, filtration-driven pressure-drop, packaging, and serviceability constraints. Detailed internal layout, component list, and absolute temperature/flow targets are omitted due to confidentiality.

Problem

  • Objective: keep cabinet bulk outlet air within a defined limit under continuous operation while maintaining margin to critical electronics temperature limits.
  • Primary risk: localized hotspots can exceed limits even when bulk cabinet air appears acceptable (distribution + recirculation effects).
  • Environmental constraints: filtered intake required to mitigate water/salt/particulate ingress → added system resistance and serviceability requirements.
  • Integration constraints: airflow architecture developed within real cabinet layout boundaries, including adjacent hardware, mounting surfaces, wiring clearance, and maintenance access (filter replacement) in a production environment.
Reliability Pressure Drop Filtration Ingress DFM/Integration Hotspots

Requirements & Constraints

  • Thermal: bulk outlet air must remain below a specified limit at worst-case ambient; critical electronics must remain below rated temperature with margin.
  • Airflow: deliver required flow at the true operating point (system resistance + filtration), not nameplate fan flow.
  • Ingress protection: intake and exhaust locations selected based on surrounding equipment, available clearances, and natural convection direction, favoring bottom intake and upper exhaust to support upward heat removal while avoiding interference with adjacent subsystems.
  • Serviceability: filters accessible for replacement and inspection; design supports maintenance without cabinet rework.
Note on confidentiality: Specific component part numbers, heat loads by module, airflow targets, and absolute temperature limits are intentionally not listed. The focus here is the engineering workflow, conservative assumptions, and verification approach.
Notation (used below)
\[ \dot{Q}=\text{heat rate (W)},\quad \dot{m}=\text{mass flow (kg/s)},\quad \dot{V}=\text{volumetric flow (m}^3\!/s) \] \[ c_p=\text{specific heat},\quad \rho=\text{air density},\quad \Delta T = T_{out}-T_{in},\quad \Delta P=\text{pressure drop} \]

Approach

1) First-principles airflow sizing

Sized required airflow using a steady-state energy balance on cabinet air (heat load + allowable bulk temperature rise). Included fan electrical power as part of the effective thermal load where applicable, and documented modeling assumptions (steady state, constant properties, negligible KE/PE).

Equations used
\[ \dot{Q}_{tot} \approx \dot{m}\,c_p\,(T_{out}-T_{in}) \] \[ \dot{m} = \rho\,\dot{V}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \dot{V} \approx \frac{\dot{Q}_{tot}}{\rho\,c_p\,\Delta T} \] \[ \dot{Q}_{tot} = \dot{Q}_{electronics} + \alpha\,\dot{W}_{fan} \]
\(\alpha\) captures whether fan electrical input is treated as an added cabinet heat load (often \(\alpha\approx 1\) for conservative sizing when fan is inside/thermally coupled).

2) System resistance modeling (ΔP vs V̇)

Estimated cabinet pressure drop by combining filtration losses, internal restrictions, and exit losses to form a system curve. Fan selection was performed at the coupled operating point (fan curve ∩ system curve), with conservative allowances for filter loading. CFD remains an optional refinement to further de-risk airflow distribution and hotspot behavior prior to full hardware build.

Equations used
\[ \Delta P_{sys}(\dot{V}) = \Delta P_{filter}(\dot{V}) + \Delta P_{minor}(\dot{V}) + \Delta P_{friction}(\dot{V}) \] \[ \Delta P_{minor} \approx \sum K_i\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\dot{V}}{A_i}\right)^2 \] \[ \Delta P_{friction} \approx \sum \left(f\,\frac{L}{D}\right)\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\dot{V}}{A}\right)^2 \] \[ \text{Operating point: }\;\Delta P_{fan}(\dot{V}^*)=\Delta P_{sys}(\dot{V}^*) \] \[ \Delta P_{filter,dirty} \approx SF \cdot \Delta P_{filter,clean} \]
\(K_i\) = loss coefficients (bends, contractions, screens, exits). \(SF\) = filter loading safety factor (e.g., “dirty filter” allowance).

3) Fan + filter integration under ingress constraints

  • Selected and positioned intake/exhaust features to support environmental ingress goals while maintaining serviceability.
  • Integrated filtration to control particulate exposure while accounting for added system resistance in fan selection.
  • Ensured the cooling architecture remains manufacturable (mounting strategy, sealing surfaces, filter changeout access).

4) Hotspot bounding using measured correlation (MCU vs bulk air)

  • Used instrumented measurements from a comparable configuration with sensors placed in cabinet air and near the critical electronics.
  • Observed a repeatable offset between local cabinet air and MCU temperature that varied by operating mode.
  • Designed to the upper bound of the observed offset as a conservative basis to maintain margin to electronics temperature limits.
Equations used
\[ T_{MCU} \approx T_{bulk} + \Delta T_{offset} \] \[ T_{MCU,design} = T_{bulk,design} + \Delta T_{offset,max} \] \[ \text{Margin} = T_{MCU,limit} - T_{MCU,design} \]
\(\Delta T_{offset,max}\) is the conservative bound observed across operating modes (captures hotspot risk from non-uniform flow / recirculation).

Plenum Design (In Progress)

  • Purpose: transition from fan outlet to filter face while improving flow uniformity and minimizing added pressure loss.
  • Design intent: reduce jetting and recirculation, distribute flow across the full filter area, and prevent bypass leakage around the filter.
  • Integration: sealed interfaces (gaskets), robust mounting, and maintenance-friendly access for filter replacement.
  • Materials: corrosion-resistant construction appropriate for the operating environment; stiffness and vibration considered.
  • Ingress: geometry and sealing chosen to support environmental protection goals without compromising serviceability.
Useful relationships (design checks)
\[ \Delta P_{added} \approx K_{added}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\dot{V}}{A}\right)^2 \] \[ \text{Face velocity: } V_{face}=\frac{\dot{V}}{A_{filter}} \]
Practically: keep \(K_{added}\) small (avoid sharp turns / sudden contractions), and reduce \(V_{face}\) spikes to improve filter utilization and limit pressure loss.

Verification Plan (Planned)

  • Instrumentation: inlet/outlet air temperatures, internal air mapping near suspected hot zones, and MCU temperature via onboard sensor or surface thermocouple.
  • Test conditions: worst-case ambient and load cases; repeat runs at multiple operating modes; include filtration loading sensitivity.
  • Acceptance: demonstrate compliance with bulk outlet air requirement and maintain margin to critical electronics temperature limits.
  • Optional CFD: used prior to full build if hotspot margin is tight or to refine airflow distribution and internal temperature gradients.
What gets validated
\[ \Delta T_{meas}=T_{out,meas}-T_{in,meas} \] \[ \dot{Q}_{inferred}\approx \rho\,\dot{V}_{meas}\,c_p\,\Delta T_{meas} \] \[ \text{Pass if: } T_{bulk,out}\le T_{bulk,limit}\;\;\text{and}\;\; T_{MCU}\le T_{MCU,limit}\;(\text{with margin}) \]
You can use measured \(\dot{V}\) (or inferred from fan operating point + \(\Delta P\)) to cross-check that the cabinet is running at the intended operating point.
