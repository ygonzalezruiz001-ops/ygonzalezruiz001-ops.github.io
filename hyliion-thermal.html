Thermal Management for LV Cabinet

Hyliion • 800 kW Containerized Generator • Forced Convection • CFD • Fan/System Curves

The LV cabinet had a brutal thermal constraint: without forced convection, worst-case internal temperatures were projected to reach ~290°C. The requirement was <55°C — inside a tight spaceclaim — in a marine environment where salt + humidity mean filtration and higher pressure drop. I owned the airflow + fan selection workflow end-to-end: from first-principles sizing → converged fan power coupling → CFD/system curve → design changes for real-world fabrication.

Problem

  • Thermal risk: no forced convection → projected temperatures approaching ~290°C (unacceptable).
  • Requirement: keep cabinet below 55°C while respecting strict packaging constraints.
  • Marine environment: cannot freely ingest ambient air — filtration needed for moisture + salt aerosols, increasing system pressure drop.
  • Design reality: airflow must be compatible with fabrication, serviceability, and available electrical power.

Approach

1) First-principles airflow sizing + fan heat coupling

Starting from the heat load (Q ≈ 200 W) and temperature constraint, I computed the required mass flow using basic energy balance (Q = ṁ · cp · ΔT) and converted to volumetric flow rate (V̇). Because the fan’s electrical power ultimately becomes heat inside/near the cabinet, I solved the coupled problem iteratively: required flow → fan power → updated Q → updated flow… until it converged.

2) System curve (ΔP vs V̇) using CFD, anchored by experiment

The real challenge isn’t just flow — it’s flow under restriction. Filtration + tight internal ducting increases ΔP, so I built a system curve for the cabinet. To de-risk CFD assumptions, I used an earlier 200 kW cabinet as a physical test article: measured pressure + flow to establish the curve, then verified trends in CFD. I then applied the validated workflow to the 800 kW cabinet design.

3) Fan selection at the operating point + design changes to make it real

  • Plotted the cabinet system curve and overlaid supplier fan curves to select the fan at the true operating point.
  • Accounted for filter ΔP (salt/humidity media) and inlet/outlet losses in the total pressure budget.
  • Checked fan power draw vs available electrical supply and ensured the thermal model included fan heat add-back.
  • Updated cabinet design with new inlet/outlet openings and mounting strategy to fit the constrained spaceclaim.
  • Result: design predictions indicated the cabinet could meet the <55°C requirement.

What I’d Test On Hardware

  • Instrument the cabinet: thermocouples at hotspots + inlet/outlet air temps, plus cabinet internal air temperature mapping.
  • Measure performance: pressure taps across filter + fan, and airflow measurement (anemometer/grid or inline flow device).
  • Confirm model assumptions: compare measured ΔP–V̇ to predicted system curve; adjust CFD loss coefficients if needed.
  • Acceptance criteria: demonstrate <55°C at defined ambient + heat load with margin, and document repeatability.
