Overview

Designed and prototyped a complete hydrogen generation system for laboratory research using alkaline electrolysis. The system operates at ~400 psi and requires strict isolation of hydrogen and oxygen gases through leak-tight design, material compatibility, and safety-first validation.

System Context

The system uses alkaline electrolysis to split water:

2H₂O + electrical energy → 2H₂ + O₂

  • KOH electrolyte enables ion conduction between electrodes.
  • Hydrogen (cathode) and oxygen (anode) must remain fully isolated and captured separately.
  • Manufacturing implication: pressure integrity + leak-tight joints + chemical compatibility + disciplined testing.

Materials & Design Specifications

  • Stainless steel for electrodes/base plates (corrosion resistance in alkaline environment).
  • PEEK protective housing for chemical + thermal stability.
  • PTFE (Teflon) seals/gaskets after thermal review showed rubber approaching melt risk (PTFE rated ~400°C).
  • Stainless fasteners to prevent galvanic corrosion in alkaline service.
  • Press-fit threaded inserts in plastic components for repeatable assembly without stripping.
  • 6061-T6 aluminum where non-wetted for machinability and weight control.

Design + Prototyping Process

  • Built three full CAD iterations in SolidWorks, driven by prototype testing and manufacturability feedback.
  • 300+ part assembly: pressure vessels, fluid manifolds, separation chambers, sensor integration, supports.
  • Applied DFM/DFA from the start:
    • Geometry simplification to minimize machining complexity
    • Standardized fastener sizes to reduce SKU complexity
    • Fillets over sharp corners to reduce stress concentrations
    • Injection-molding-aware plastic design (draft, undercut avoidance, consistent wall thickness)

Hands-on Fabrication

  • Fabricated prototype components using CNC mills, drill presses, band saws, laser cutters, waterjet, and hand tools.
  • Learned what features drive headaches for machinists (multiple setups, difficult fixturing, unrealistic tolerances).
  • Converted fabrication lessons directly back into CAD to reduce cost, lead time, and assembly friction.

Core philosophy developed: Design with the machinist in mind.

Safety-Critical Lesson: Gas Permeability

A custom fitting was designed to integrate a hydrogen sensor. To save time, it was initially produced via FDM 3D printing. Before hydrogen testing, a safety pressure test was performed with compressed air and soap-bubble leak detection.

  • Result: leaks detected.
  • Root cause: FDM layer porosity → gas permeability (hydrogen would leak even more).
  • Fix: redesigned and machined a replacement from 6061-T6 aluminum.
  • Validation: pressure tested aluminum version → zero leaks (remains in service).

Takeaway: material properties are as critical as geometry—optimize for the environment (pressure, temperature, chemical exposure, permeability) and verify by test.

Manufacturing Mindset

  • Designed parts to minimize CNC setups and complex fixturing where possible.
  • Balanced functionality with manufacturability to reduce cost and schedule risk.
  • Created production-ready drawings with GD&T (ASME Y14.5) and material specifications for external shops.
