Project Overview

Leading structural design and analysis for containerized megawatt-scale energy generator systems from concept through detailed design review. Responsible for frame architecture optimization, AWS D1.1 weld stress analysis, supplier coordination, and DFM.

Structural Optimization & Part Elimination

  • Applied a “every part must earn its keep” philosophy: eliminated non-load-carrying brackets/secondary members.
  • Confirmed load paths remained valid after removals using von Mises + strain energy density reviews.
  • Reran FEA without eliminated parts to ensure margins held for 5g transport and 3g operational cases.

Vibration & Dynamic Response

  • Raised minimum natural frequency 8 Hz → 31 Hz through targeted X-bracing and gusseting.
  • Purpose: avoid resonance with rotating equipment operating speeds (frequency avoidance requirement).

Weld Analysis & DFM

  • Extracted forces/moments from ANSYS, calculated weld stresses for fillet + CJP, checked vs AWS D1.1 allowables.
  • When weld capacity was insufficient, redistributed loads using stiffeners (gussets/bracing) instead of “just increasing weld size.”
  • Worked with fabrication partner to confirm process feasibility (CJP capability, access, fixturing, lead times).

Supplier Coordination

  • Released drawings/cut lists/material specs with clear requirements and tolerances.
  • Evaluated alternate suppliers for structural steel procurement (lead time, cost, certs).
  • Managed schedule pressure to close analysis + design review readiness under tight timelines.

Problem Statement

Drug development was bottlenecked by rotovap cycle time (48 hours). Root cause was economic: continuous electric vacuum pumps were cost-prohibitive.

Root Cause Reframe

  • Shifted from “can we afford pumps?” to “how do we achieve vacuum within startup constraints?”
  • Designed solution around low-cost operability, not expensive equipment.

Solution: Water Aspirator Vacuum

  • Used venturi effect: high-velocity water flow creates low pressure region to draw vacuum.
  • Optimized tubing diameters, sealed leaks, validated leak-tightness via pressure testing.
  • Created simple SOP so the team could run it daily with minimal training.

Why it Works

  • Lower absolute pressure reduces boiling point → faster evaporation without excessive heat (important for sensitive compounds).

Validation

  • Daily use over 12+ months with repeatable 4-hour cycles across repeated experiments.

System Context

Alkaline electrolysis (KOH electrolyte) splits water into hydrogen and oxygen. Safety requires complete isolation of gases, leak-tight connections, and compatibility with alkaline chemistry and hydrogen service.

Materials & Design Choices

  • Stainless steel in wetted/alkaline regions (corrosion resistance).
  • PEEK housing for chemical/thermal stability; PTFE seals for high temperature rating.
  • Stainless fasteners to prevent galvanic corrosion; inserts for repeatable assembly in plastics.
  • Design pressure ~400 psi (validated via analysis + testing).

DFM/DFA + Hands-on Fabrication

  • Designed with future injection molding in mind (draft, consistent wall thickness, undercut avoidance).
  • Fabricated prototypes (CNC mill, drill press, band saw, laser/waterjet, tapping/finishing).
  • Converted machining reality into better CAD: “design with the machinist in mind.”

Safety-Critical Lesson: Gas Permeability

  • 3D-printed (FDM) sensor fitting leaked during air pressure test (soap bubble detection).
  • Root cause: layer porosity → permeability (hydrogen would be worse) → unacceptable hazard.
  • Redesigned and machined replacement from 6061-T6 → zero leaks, remains in service.
