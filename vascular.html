1) Catheter Support System Redesign

The existing workflow required awkward handling and multiple operators. I identified usability issues, proposed a redesign direction, and developed a mechanical support fixture concept to improve repeatability and reduce operator burden.

  • Identified ergonomic/procedural limitations in the current workflow and proposed a redesign approach adopted by the team (development continued beyond my internship).
  • Designed a fully mechanical catheter support fixture intended to improve handling, reduce required operators from 2 → 1, and minimize catheter motion during tissue capture.
  • Completed 7 design iterations; built prototypes using 3D-printed PLA and machined stainless components.
  • Performed in-house machining operations (drilling, milling, tapping, bandsawing) and assembled prototypes for evaluation.
  • Applied DFM/DFA and Lean thinking to simplify geometry, reduce assembly steps, and improve manufacturability.

2) Pediatric Catheter Feasibility Study

Initiated a feasibility study to assess whether reduced-diameter catheter concepts could maintain safe and effective suction performance. Focused on experimental testing and engineering recommendations.

  • Designed and executed vacuum experiments to evaluate suction performance as tube diameter was reduced (8 Fr down to 6 Fr).
  • Built custom test prototypes using stainless coiled tubing and fixture components to enable repeatable testing.
  • Demonstrated via testing that pediatric-compatible designs maintained effective delta-P for suction (measured ~1.6–2.3 inHg in testing).
  • Delivered recommendations and documented future modifications, including potential geometric optimizations at the tip.

3) Rotary Evaporator Vacuum Bring-Up

The rotovap was not achieving vacuum consistently, which prevented normal evaporation performance. I brought up a reliable vacuum configuration and documented the setup so the lab could repeat it.

  • Identified that rotovap runs were bottlenecked by the system not achieving vacuum, preventing normal evaporation performance.
  • Implemented and verified a water-aspirator vacuum setup to reliably pull vacuum and stabilize operation.
  • Restored expected process performance—reducing typical cycle time from ~48 hours to ~4 hours and enabling higher throughput.
  • Documented the setup and operating procedure so results were repeatable across users.
